## Overview
Written by Sam.

[[Etemor/Player Characters/Silas Silvertongue|Silas Silvertongue]] was born in the poorest quarter of a mining village called [[Etemor/Material Plane/Alideus/Curan Kingdom/Brittlefront|Brittlefront]]. The mine was the lifeblood of the settlement, and like most boys born there, his future was decided before he could speak. His father worked long days underground and arranged for Silas to join him when he came of age. His mother never saw that day—she died giving birth to him, a loss that hung quietly over their small hovel of a home for years.

The mine, however, was never Silas’s calling. He lacked the patience for hard labour and the discipline for the long shifts beneath the earth. Instead, he discovered something else he was far better at: talking. Even as a boy, he had a way of convincing people of things—especially authority figures. His bosses often believed him when he claimed his father knew he had missed lessons or shifts, even when that was far from the truth. It was a talent that would grow sharper with time.

The real education of Silas’s life came not from the mines but from the village tavern, [[The Brass Tooth Inn]].

For years, Silas spent nearly every evening there. At first he simply observed, sitting quietly in a corner booth with a cheap drink, watching the rhythm of people coming and going. Travellers, labourers, merchants, thieves, soldiers—everyone eventually passed through those doors. Silas listened. He studied how people spoke when they were proud, how their voices changed when they were lying, and how a little alcohol loosened even the tightest lips.

Over time, he moved closer to the centre of the action: from the booths to a lone table, and eventually to a permanent seat at the bar itself.

Silas chose his place carefully—right in the middle, where the most seats surrounded him. From there he could watch everyone.

The pattern rarely failed.

A stranger would enter and sit at the end of the bar. Silas would acknowledge them with a small nod or tilt of his head—nothing intrusive, just enough to signal friendliness. After a while, he would offer to buy them a drink. Soon, the stranger would slide down the bar to sit beside him.

Then the real work began.

Hours later, after drinks and gentle conversation, Silas would know more about that person than their closest friends ever had. Secrets spilled easily to him: debts, crimes, regrets, ambitions. Silas had a way of making people feel understood, and in return, they told him everything.

People came and went, but Silas remained.

Eventually, he moved from behind the barstool to behind the bar itself. Through patience and quiet manipulation, he worked his way up until he became the tavern’s manager. The position suited him perfectly. He kept the tavern profitable for its owners—but he also made sure his own pockets were never empty.

Over time, the tavern developed a reputation.

Petty criminals, smugglers, and thieves began to frequent it. Not because Silas welcomed crime, exactly—but because he understood the people involved in it. He listened to them, judged them, and occasionally helped them. Silas had developed his own quiet code. If he believed someone was beyond redemption, he turned them away. If he thought someone deserved another chance, he offered advice, protection, or sometimes simply silence.

In return, people owed him favours. Many people. Powerful people.

Eventually, local law enforcement noticed the pattern.

They also noticed that Silas seemed to know far more about the village’s criminal underworld than any ordinary tavern keeper should.

One day, they approached him with an offer.

They knew criminals used his tavern as a gathering place. They knew he turned a blind eye to certain activities. But instead of arresting him, they proposed a deal: Silas would report any major criminals who came through his doors. In return, the authorities would overlook the smaller dealings happening under his roof.

Silas agreed—but in his own way.

He fed the authorities just enough information to keep them satisfied while continuing to protect the people he believed could change. In his mind, he was balancing the scales of justice better than the law ever could.

During these years, Silas also built a family life. He married, and together they had a son named **[[Etemor/NPCs/Cassian Silvertongue|Cassian]]**.

For a time, things seemed stable.

But when Cassian turned twelve, everything began to unravel.

Silas’s wife disappeared without warning or explanation. No body was ever found, and no clues surfaced. The loss left a hollow space in Silas’s life, one he tried to fill by burying himself even deeper in his work at the tavern.

Cassian felt the absence differently.

Without his mother and with his father constantly working, the boy grew distant and angry. He spent more time on the streets, falling in with rough crowds and learning the same tricks of crime that Silas spent his nights listening to.

At first Silas paid little attention. He believed hardship built character—after all, he himself had grown up the same way.

But Cassian didn’t simply survive the streets.

He mastered them.

As the years passed, Cassian rose through the ranks of the local underworld until he eventually became the leader of a gang called [[The Dust Skulls]]. The same instincts for reading people that Silas possessed had clearly passed to his son—but where Silas used them to manage and manipulate, Cassian used them to dominate.

All the while, Silas continued walking his dangerous line between criminals and the law.

Eventually, that balance collapsed.

One night, authorities stormed the tavern to confront Silas about his lack of cooperation. They suspected he was withholding information and protecting criminals far more than he admitted. The argument grew heated as officers pressed him for names.

Then, about twenty minutes later, the door burst open again.

Cassian entered—flanked by armed men.

Someone had tipped him off that his father was secretly working with the authorities to dismantle his criminal empire.

Chaos followed.

Steel clashed, gunshots rang out, and the tavern that had been Silas’s entire world turned into a battlefield. By the time the fighting ended, several criminals and officers lay dead, and the building itself was burning to the ground.

As the flames spread, Silas and Cassian faced each other across the burning room.

Silas called out to him—called him his son.

Cassian’s response was cold and final.

“I am no one’s son.”

He fled into the night with the surviving members of his gang, leaving the tavern to burn behind him.

Silas never saw Cassian again.

With his home destroyed and his past laid bare, Silas left the village for good. Now he wanders from place to place, trying to do good wherever he can—offering advice, helping strangers, and using the very same silver tongue that once built his reputation.

Not for profit.             

Not for power.

But in the hope that somewhere along the road, he might finally earn redemption for the life he left behind—and perhaps one day face the son who swore to kill him.
